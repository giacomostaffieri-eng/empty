# The Monsters' Supper — Scripts for your voice recordings

*No spoilers on this page. You can read it safely.*

---

## 1. Rules slideshow — voice-over

Play `2 - For the table/RULES - slideshow.pdf` and read one block per slide. Speak slowly and smile: this is the "easy" part.

**Slide 1 — Title**
> Welcome to *The Monsters' Supper*. Tonight you are not guests. You are monsters. And one of you is a murderer. Before we start, two minutes on how to play. It's easy, I promise.

**Slide 2 — What is this game?**
> Tonight we are all classic monsters: vampires, ghosts, witches, giants. A famous monster hunter came to dinner with us. At midnight the lights went out, and he was murdered. The killer is sitting at this table. Our job is to find out who.

**Slide 3 — Two teams**
> There are two teams. Team Killers has two players: the Murderer, who did it, and the Accomplice, who helped. They know each other. They want us to vote for the wrong person. Everyone else, six of us, is Team Investigators. We want to vote for the Murderer. One thing to remember: if we vote for the Accomplice, we lose too.

**Slide 4 — Your file**
> Everyone got a PDF with their name on it. Inside: the rules, two big screens that say YES and NO, who your monster is, your secret, your lines, two activities, and at the very end, your secret team.

**Slide 5 — The secret team**
> Read your secret team once, alone, when I tell you. Then go back to page one and leave it there. Never say your team out loud. Never show those pages to anyone. Not even as a joke. If you're innocent, don't bother saying so: the killers will say exactly the same thing.

**Slide 6 — How the night goes**
> First, introductions. Going around the table, everyone reads their "Read out loud" box and becomes their monster. Then we play four chapters. At the end we vote.

**Slide 7 — One chapter = 3 steps**
> Every chapter works the same way. One: the clue holder reads two clue cards, and we do what the card says. Two: going around the table, everyone reads their line for that chapter. Three: we talk. We ask questions, we accuse, we defend ourselves. About ten minutes, then the next chapter.

**Slide 8 — What is true?**
> This part is important. The clue cards are always true. The lines in your file are always true. Your secret is true. But anything people say while talking can be a lie. The killers will lie. A lot.

**Slide 9 — The YES / NO screen**
> Some activities let you ask someone a YES or NO question. If someone asks you, hold your phone against your chest, scroll to YES or NO, and show it only to the person who asked, for two seconds. Then scroll back. And here is the golden rule: on the YES/NO screen, everybody tells the truth. Even the Murderer. It's the only thing in the game you can't lie about.

**Slide 10 — Good question, bad question**
> Ask about places, times and things. "Were you in the kitchen at midnight?" Good. "Did you touch the window?" Good. "Are you the Murderer?" No. You can never ask about teams. If your file doesn't have the answer, just answer as honestly as you can.

**Slide 11 — Activities**
> Everyone has two activities. That's it, just two. Each one works once. You can use at most one per chapter. Say its name out loud, then do what the page says. You don't need to remember anything: it's all written on the page.

**Slide 12 — Example: do it like this**
> An example, with two monsters who are *not* in tonight's game. It's Chapter 2. The Mummy thinks the Werewolf is lying. The Mummy says out loud: "I use Secret Question! Werewolf, were you on the balcony at midnight?" The Werewolf scrolls to YES and shows it only to the Mummy. Now only the Mummy knows the truth. She can tell everyone, or keep it for later. That's all it takes.

**Slide 13 — Don't do this**
> Five things you must never do. Don't open someone else's file. Clue holder: never scroll past the pumpkin card back. Never lie on the YES/NO screen. Never say your team out loud. And don't show your YES or NO to the whole table, only to the person who asked. Everything else, the lies, the bluffs, the alliances, the drama, is the game.

**Slide 14 — The Final Vote**
> After Chapter 4 we vote. Everyone accuses someone in one sentence, or passes. Then everyone writes one name in their phone's Notes, in secret. On three, we all flip our phones. Most votes is the accused. If it's a tie, the tied players get thirty seconds each to defend themselves, then we vote with our hands. Still a tie? The killers win.

**Slide 15 — Who wins?**
> If the accused is the Murderer, the Investigators win. If it's anyone else, even the Accomplice, the Killers win. Then the killers show their secret page, and we read the solution together.

**Slide 16 — Let the night begin**
> Now open your file. Go to the last pages, read your secret team, alone. Then go back to page one. And now, listen carefully…
>
> *(Start the intro audio.)*

## 2. Intro audio — "The night of the murder"

Record this after the slideshow, then play it while everyone has their phone on page 1. Speak low and slow, like a ghost story by candlelight. Add a creaking door, wind or thunder in the background if you like.

> *(slowly, almost whispering)*
>
> Every year, on the thirty-first of October, when the living lock their doors… the monsters come out to eat.
>
> The Vampire leaves his villa. The Witch leaves her broom by the door. The Ghost floats in through the wall, the Creature crawls out of the water, and the Golem — the giant of clay — sets the table. This year, the Monsters' Supper is at his house. Here. Tonight.
>
> *(pause)*
>
> But this year, someone else knocked on the door. A man in a black coat, with a silver cross and a heavy black book under his arm. **Professor Abraham Van Helsing.** The most famous monster hunter in the world.
>
> "I come in peace," he said. "I want to sign a treaty. Humans and monsters. No more stakes, no more silver bullets. At midnight, in the study, we sign." And he put the black book on the table. *The Book of Endings.* One page for every monster in this house… and how to destroy them.
>
> Nobody touched their food after that.
>
> *(pause)*
>
> At twenty to midnight, the Professor locked himself in the study. At one minute to midnight, a scream tore through the house. Then… every light went out.
>
> *(long pause, three seconds of silence)*
>
> Four minutes of darkness. When the lights came back, everything looked the same. The music. The candles. The crema catalana. At five past midnight, the Golem knocked on the study door. No answer. He knocked again. Then he broke it down.
>
> Professor Van Helsing was dead in his chair.
>
> *(slower)*
>
> The door was locked from the inside. The key was in his pocket. Nobody could have gone in. Nobody could have come out. And yet… somebody did.
>
> One of you killed him. One of you helped. The other six were sitting right next to them… and didn't notice a thing.
>
> *(normal voice, a bit faster)*
>
> Now, monsters. Look at the person next to you. Smile. Going around the table, starting with your host, read your "Read out loud" box on page 8. Show us who you are.
>
> And remember… **everything the clues say is true. People are not.**
>
> Happy Halloween.
>
> *(thunder, or an evil laugh, then stop the audio)*

## 3. Order of the night (cheat sheet for you)

1. **Before the dinner:** send each person **only their own** `Pack - Name.pdf` in a private chat. Don't open the others.
2. **Clue holder:** you (or anyone) keep `CLUES - for the clue holder.pdf` open on a phone, stopped on the first pumpkin card back.
3. **Play the slideshow** with your voice-over. About 6 minutes.
4. **Everyone reads their secret team** (pages 13–14), alone, then goes back to page 1.
5. **Play the intro audio.** About 3 minutes.
6. **Introductions:** going around the table, starting with you, everyone reads their page 8 box.
7. **Chapters 1 to 4:** 2 clue cards → everyone's line → talk and activities. About 10 minutes each.
8. **Final vote** → the killers reveal → open `SOLUTION - open only after the vote.pdf` and read it out loud.
9. Bonus: vote "Best Liar" and "Best Performance".

Total time: about 1 hour 15 minutes.
