# The Monsters' Supper — Scripts for your voice recordings

*No spoilers on this page. You can read it safely.*

---

## 1. Rules slideshow — voice-over

Play `2 - For the table/RULES - slideshow.pdf` and read one block per slide. Speak slowly and smile: this is the easy part. About 8 minutes.

**Slide 1 — Title**
> Welcome to *The Monsters' Supper*. Tonight you are not guests. You are monsters. And one of you is a murderer. Before we start, a few minutes on how to play. It's easy, I promise.

**Slide 2 — What is this game?**
> Tonight we are all classic monsters: vampires, ghosts, gorgons, even a Martian. A famous monster hunter came to dinner with us. At midnight the lights went out, and he was murdered. The killer is sitting at this table. Our job is to find out who.

**Slide 3 — The house**
> This is where it happened: my house, on Halloween night. The balcony, the study, the living room, the hallway, the bathroom and the kitchen. You'll find this map on page 8 of your file. When people tell you where they were, look at the map. Could they really have been there? Could they have seen something?

**Slide 4 — Two teams**
> There are two teams. Team Killers has two players: the Murderer, who did it, and the Accomplice, who helped. They know each other. They want us to vote for the wrong person. Everyone else, six of us, is Team Investigators. We want to vote for the Murderer. Remember: if we vote for the Accomplice, we lose too.

**Slide 5 — Your file**
> Everyone got a PDF with their name on it. Inside: the rules, the map, a gallery of all the guests, two big YES and NO screens, your monster with your drawing, your secret, your lines, four activities, and at the very end, your secret team.

**Slide 6 — The secret team**
> In a moment you'll read your secret team, alone. Then go back to page one and leave it there. Never say your team out loud. Never show those pages to anyone. Not even as a joke. And don't bother saying "I'm innocent": the killers will say exactly the same thing.

**Slide 7 — How the night goes**
> First, introductions. Going around the table, everyone shows their drawing on page 12, reads their "Read out loud" box and becomes their monster. Then we play four chapters, about fifteen minutes each. At the end we vote. About an hour and a half of murder.

**Slide 8 — One chapter = 5 steps**
> Every chapter works the same way, in five steps. One: clues. The clue holder reads two clue cards, and we do what the card says. Two: lines. Going around the table, everyone reads their line for that chapter. Three: your turn. Going around again, everyone gets one free question and can use one activity. Four: open talk, four minutes, everyone can speak. Five: point. On three, everyone points at their suspect, and the person with the most fingers on them gets thirty seconds to defend themselves. Then the next chapter starts.

**Slide 9 — Who talks when**
> To avoid chaos, here's who talks and when. During the clues and the lines, only the person reading talks. Everyone else listens. On your turn, only you and the person you're asking talk. Everyone else waits for their own turn. In open talk, everyone can speak, but one at a time: raise your hand, and the clue holder points at who goes next. The clue holder also keeps the time.

**Slide 10 — The free question**
> On your turn you can always ask one free question, without using any activity. Ask anyone anything, out loud. "Where were you at midnight?" "Why did you leave the table?" They answer out loud, thirty seconds max, and you get one follow-up question. But careful: with a free question, people are allowed to lie.

**Slide 11 — What is true?**
> So what can you trust? The clue cards are always true. The lines in your file are always true. Your secret is true. And the YES/NO screen is always true. Everything people say out loud can be a lie. The killers will lie. A lot.

**Slide 12 — The YES / NO screen**
> Some activities make someone answer YES or NO. If someone uses one on you, hold your phone against your chest, scroll to YES or NO, and show it only to the person who asked, for two seconds. Then scroll back. And here's the golden rule: on the YES/NO screen, everybody tells the truth. Even the Murderer. It's the only thing in the game you can't lie about.

**Slide 13 — Good question, bad question**
> Ask about places, times and things. "Were you in the kitchen at midnight?" Good. "Did you touch the window?" Good. "Are you the Murderer?" No. You can never ask about teams. The truth about your own night is written on your last page, so you always know what to answer. If it isn't written, answer as honestly as you can.

**Slide 14 — Activities**
> Everyone has four activities, one for each chapter. Each one works once. You use them on your turn: say the name out loud, then do what the page says. You don't need to remember anything, it's all written there. Two exceptions: a BLOCK activity can be used any time someone targets you, and the vote activities are used at the final vote.

**Slide 15 — Example: a perfect turn**
> Here's an example, with two monsters who are *not* in tonight's game. It's the Mummy's turn, so everyone else is quiet. Free question: "Werewolf, where were you at midnight?" The Werewolf says: "In the kitchen, eating." Maybe a lie. Follow-up: "Who saw you?" "…Nobody." Now the Mummy uses an activity: "I use Secret Question! Werewolf, were you on the balcony at midnight?" The Werewolf has to tell the truth, and secretly shows YES to the Mummy. Caught! Only the Mummy knows… for now. She can tell everyone in open talk, or keep it for later. Then it's the next player's turn.

**Slide 16 — Don't do this**
> Five things you must never do. Don't talk during someone else's turn. Don't open someone else's file. Clue holder: never scroll past the pumpkin card back. Never lie on the YES/NO screen. And never say your team out loud. Everything else, the lies, the bluffs, the alliances, the drama, is the game.

**Slide 17 — The Final Vote**
> After Chapter 4 we vote. Everyone accuses someone in one sentence, or passes. Then everyone writes one name in their phone's Notes, in secret. On three, we all flip our phones. Most votes is the accused. If it's a tie, the tied players get thirty seconds each to defend themselves, then we vote with our hands. Still a tie? The killers win.

**Slide 18 — Who wins?**
> If the accused is the Murderer, the Investigators win. If it's anyone else, even the Accomplice, the Killers win. Then the killers reveal themselves, and we read the solution together.

**Slide 19 — Let the night begin**
> Now open your file. Go to the last two pages, pages 20 and 21, and read your secret team, alone. Then go back to page one. And now, listen carefully…
>
> *(Start the intro audio.)*

## 2. Intro audio — "The night of the murder"

Record this and play it after the slideshow, while everyone has their phone on page 1. Speak low and slow, like a ghost story by candlelight. Add a creaking door, wind or thunder in the background if you like. About 3 minutes.

> *(slowly, almost whispering)*
>
> Every year, on the thirty-first of October, when the living lock their doors… the monsters come out to eat.
>
> The Vampire leaves his villa. The Martian parks her flying saucer on the roof. Medusa checks her snakes in the mirror. The Ghost floats in through the wall, the Creature crawls out of the water, and Beetlejuice — the ghost with the most — sets the table. This year, the Monsters' Supper is at his house. Here. Tonight.
>
> And for the first time, the smallest monster of all is here too: little Alma, half bat, half ghost. Tonight, she's the only one with a perfect alibi.
>
> *(pause)*
>
> But this year, someone else knocked on the door. A man in a black coat, with a silver cross and a heavy black book under his arm. **Professor Abraham Van Helsing.** The most famous monster hunter in the world.
>
> "I come in peace," he said. "I want to sign a treaty. Humans and monsters. No more stakes, no more silver bullets. At midnight, in the study, we sign." And he put the black book on the table. *The Book of Endings.* One page for every monster in this house… and how to destroy them.
>
> Nobody touched their food after that.
>
> *(pause)*
>
> At twenty to midnight, the Professor locked himself in the study. At one minute to midnight, a scream tore through the house. Then… every light went out.
>
> *(long pause, three seconds of silence)*
>
> Four minutes of darkness. When the lights came back, everything looked the same. The music. The candles. The crema catalana. At five past midnight, Beetlejuice knocked on the study door. No answer. He knocked again. Then he broke it down.
>
> Professor Van Helsing was dead in his chair.
>
> *(slower)*
>
> The door was locked from the inside. The key was in his pocket. Nobody could have gone in. Nobody could have come out. And yet… somebody did.
>
> One of you killed him. One of you helped. The other six were sitting right next to them… and didn't notice a thing.
>
> *(normal voice, a bit faster)*
>
> Now, monsters. Look at the person next to you. Smile. Going around the table, starting with your host, show us your drawing on page 12 and read your "Read out loud" box. Show us who you are.
>
> And remember… **everything the clues say is true. People are not.**
>
> Happy Halloween.
>
> *(thunder, or an evil laugh, then stop the audio)*

## 3. Order of the night (cheat sheet for you)

1. **A few days before:** send `COSTUMES - share in the group.pdf` to the WhatsApp group. Everyone sees their monster and an easy costume idea. No spoilers inside.
2. **Before the dinner:** send each person **only their own** `Pack - Name.pdf` in a private chat. Tell them: "Don't open pages 20–21 until I say so." Don't open the others yourself.
3. **Clue holder** (you, or anyone): `CLUES - for the clue holder.pdf` open on a phone, stopped on the first pumpkin, plus a phone timer.
4. **Slideshow** with your voice-over (about 8 min).
5. **Everyone reads their secret team** (pages 20–21), alone, then goes back to page 1.
6. **Intro audio** (about 3 min).
7. **Introductions:** going around the table, starting with you, everyone shows their drawing (page 12) and reads their box (about 10 min).
8. **Chapters 1 to 4**, about 15 min each:
   1. Clues: 2 cards and their effects
   2. Lines: everyone reads theirs
   3. Your turn: 1 free question + 1 activity each (about 1 min each)
   4. Open talk: 4 min, hands up
   5. Point: on three, the most-pointed player defends for 30 s
9. **Final vote** → the killers reveal themselves → open `SOLUTION - open only after the vote.pdf` and read it out loud.
10. Bonus: vote "Best Liar" and "Best Costume" (Alma is not allowed to win — it would be unfair to everyone else).

**Total: about 1 hour 45 minutes**, including the slideshow and the introductions. The game itself, from Chapter 1 to the vote, is about 1 hour 10 minutes.
